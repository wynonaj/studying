Business Processes
A set of tasks or activities that process inputs to produce desired outputs
“Work” that is done by an entity (human or information system)
“A collection of related, structured activities or tasks by people or equipment in which a specific sequence produces a service or product (serves a particular business goal) for a particular customer or customers.”
“A business process may often be visualized (modeled) as a flowchart of a sequence of activities with interleaving decision points or as a process matrix of a sequence of activities with relevance rules based on data in the process.”https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process

SLIDE 26
Fulfillment vs Procurement Invoices: How to they Differ?
Sales Invoice – term used in the Fulfillment process
A Sales Invoice is “sent” to the customer
It is a data record documenting the request for and receipt of payments from customers / clients
Can serve as proof of purchase
Vendor Invoice – term used in the Procurement process
A Vendor Invoice is “received” from the vendor
It is a request for payment to the vendor
Can serve as proof of purchase

What is Enterprise Architecture?
Broad design, plan or blueprint for a business, applications, data, technologies, enterprise, etc.
Supports optimal design and re-design of organizational structures
Enforces business process standardization and integration
Prevents “hairball architecture” from resulting
33

SLIDE 34
The “Layers” of Enterprise Architecture
Business Architecture – The business strategy, governance, organization, and key business processes.
Data Architecture – The structure of an organization’s logical and physical data assets and data management resources.
Application Architecture – A blueprint for the individual applications to be deployed, their interactions, and their relationships to the core business processes of the organization.
Technology Architecture – The logical software and hardware capabilities that are required to support the deployment of business, data, and application services. This includes IT infrastructure, middleware, networks, communications, processing, and standards.

SLIDE 45
Functional Silo Perspective
When the organizational mindset of departments, teams, individuals, etc. is to operate in isolation, placing greater focus on their goals and objectives over those of the organization.
Isolation can lead to the Silo Effect
They lose sight of organizational goals in favor of their own
Increase likelihood of task duplication across functional groups
Delays and varying perceptions of quality are possible
Ineffective inter-functional coordination and communication

SLIDE 47
Functional vs Business Process
Functional Perspective
Silos – self-contained units
Enable optimization of expertise
Good when that’s the goal, but can be bad if its not
May result unintended competition among functional units
May also cause a loss of  focus on the “Big Picture”
Business Process Perspective
Recognize that process are cross-functional
Keeps the focus on the work
Enables optimization of value creation
No loss of “Big Picture” focus

SLIDE 52
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)
A type of EIS that supports cross-functional processes
ERP I (first version)
Can support all processes, but organization purchases only the “modules” for the processes it has
Includes a common database that supports enterprise processes (single version of the “truth”)
ERP II (second version)
ERP I features along with
Real-time system access
Web-based applications
Support for interorganizational processes
